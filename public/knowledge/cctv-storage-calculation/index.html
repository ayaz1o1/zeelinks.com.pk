CCTV Storage Calculation: A Practical Retention Planning Guide

Calculate CCTV storage from camera count, bitrate, recording hours and retention, then validate the result against real NVR and HDD limits.

Why storage calculations go wrong

A camera's megapixel rating does not directly tell you how many days a hard drive will retain. Storage is driven mainly by bitrate, camera count, recording schedule and retention period. Codec, scene complexity and variable bitrate settings can also change the real result.

The basic planning method

Start with the configured bitrate for each camera. Multiply total bitrate by the number of cameras and recording time, then convert the resulting data volume into practical storage units. For mixed camera systems, calculate groups separately rather than assuming every camera uses the same bitrate.

Continuous recording vs event recording

Continuous recording makes retention easier to predict because the camera records throughout the schedule. Motion or event recording can reduce storage, but the saving depends on how busy the scene is. A gate, road or production floor can generate much more event activity than a quiet office.

Do not ignore the NVR

A large HDD does not solve an NVR limitation. Check supported HDD capacity, number of drive bays, incoming bandwidth, supported camera bitrates and the recorder's recording configuration.

Field checklist

Before finalizing storage, document camera count, resolution, codec, bitrate, FPS, recording mode, hours/day, required retention and HDD configuration. Then compare the theoretical result with the NVR's actual storage status after commissioning.
